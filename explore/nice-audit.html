How it works

1 · Encode

Recommendations become rules

Each NICE recommendation is written as a precise rule: who it applies to, what counts as met, and the time window, agreed with clinical leads.

2 · Structured data

Checked against routine records

Observations, prescriptions, appointments, questionnaires and referral dates are checked automatically, every night.

3 · Free text

Evidence found in letters

Much good practice is only written in letters. Language AI looks for it (for example the child's own views, or a sleep plan) so a missing tick box isn't counted as a failure. See the AI-on-letters demo.

4 · Sign-off

Clinicians confirm and act

Flags go to the responsible clinician to confirm or dismiss, which also trains the rules. Results feed governance reports and improvement projects.

Example rule

Standard   BP and heart rate before and after each dose change
Source     NICE NG87 recommendation 1.8.9
Applies to everyone with a dose change during titration
Met if     BP and HR recorded within 14 days before
           and 28 days after the change  (windows set locally)
Evidence   observations · home BP readings · letters (AI)
Owner      titration team · reviewed monthly

Keeping up with NICE

  • Every rule records the guideline, recommendation number and version it was built from.
  • When NICE updates a guideline (NG87 has been amended since 2018, most recently adding QbTest in 2024), affected rules are reviewed, re-tested and re-signed by a clinical lead before going live.
  • A change log shows commissioners and the CQC exactly what was measured, and when.
  • The same engine can carry local standards, shared care protocols and CQC evidence.
